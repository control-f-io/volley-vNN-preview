Fixed alarm limits treat every machine alike, so they trip too late or too often. Volley gives each asset its own memory and learns its normal: shifts, warm-ups, seasons. It is built to flag what changes early, keeps learning in operation without retraining, and tells your team in plain language why a machine is flagged.

Why “Volley”

Wever & Bray, 1930

Many neurons, firing in turn, follow a signal none of them could follow alone.

In the auditory nerve no single neuron can fire fast enough to follow a high tone. Groups of them fire in staggered volleys, and together they keep up with the signal.

Volley does the same for a factory: many small spiking units, each firing sparsely, that together follow every stream of every asset, without ever stopping.

01 · Architecture

Select any part

A spike-only stack that reads a stream.

Volley's core is the spiking stack that Control-F has measured release by release since v11 under the name ShuntFormer. It has no attention blocks: eleven levels, each a state read and a layer of threshold cells, pass whole spikes through a 512-channel stream. vNN wraps it with telemetry encoders and a memory capsule for every asset.

    02 · Streaming

    Six advantages

    Windows forget. Streams remember.

    A Transformer reads in windows. Everything older than its window is gone, and its weights stop changing once it ships. Volley reads one unbroken stream per asset, carries its state forward, forgets gradually and keeps learning. Telemetry never ends, so a model that reads a stream fits the problem.

    01

    Unbroken streams

    One step per sample, state carried forward. A stream cut anywhere fires exactly the spikes of one pass over the whole of it, so any asset can be paused, checkpointed and resumed without re-reading its history.

    Measured · v15–v16

    02

    Reads beyond its training

    Trained on windows of 2,048 bytes, the stack reads 8,192 cells back, four times further, with no new synapses, and scores 0.013 bits per byte better for it.

    Measured · v16

    03

    Forgets the way memory does

    Beyond the window a read's charge falls by a learned slope for every doubling of age: forgetting by a power law, as in human memory. 79 of 88 heads learned a slope that keeps the far past bounded at any reach.

    Measured · v16

    04

    Learns while it infers

    Fast synapses start at zero in every stream, and each arrival teaches them by a local rule in whole numbers, with no gradient and no retraining. On books their gain grows as the square root of what was read.

    Measured · v15–v16

    05

    Event-driven by design

    Whole spikes and synapses on an 8-bit grid cost 0.0008 bits per byte against floating point. A steady signal sends few spikes. The design targets edge and neuromorphic silicon. The native integer decoder and its energy audit come next.

    Design target

    06

    One model, many memories

    All assets share the slow weights, and each stream gets a small capsule of its own. That personalises the model per asset without fine-tuning, retraining or a vector database.

    03 · Industrial AI

    Per asset · per stream

    One nervous system per asset.

    Volley vNN brings the stream to the shop floor. Every pump, spindle, compressor and conveyor gets its own memory capsule. The capsule learns that asset's normal: the load cycle of its shifts, its warm-up after a start, the slow drift of its seasons. The same model answers questions about the asset in plain language.

    1. 01

      Sense

      Telemetry arrives as spike events, stream by stream, at its own rate.

    2. 02

      Predict

      Before each sample arrives, Volley predicts it from this asset's own history.

    3. 03

      Surprise

      The bits a sample costs are its surprise, measured against this asset's normal rather than a fleet threshold.

    4. 04

      Teach

      The arrival teaches the fast synapses in whole numbers. Inference is training.

    5. 05

      Answer

      Operators ask in language, and the answer is read from the same live state.

    The principle

    Surprise is the anomaly score. The error spikes that teach Volley are the same ones that find what changed, often long before a static alarm limit trips.
